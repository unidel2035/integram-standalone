Dmitry Losinets

Abstract
This work summarizes the results obtained in papers [1–5] and presents an at-
tempt to construct a unified system in which the parameters of a hydrodynamic
medium, modeling the quantum vacuum (QV), are defined. This model allows a
consistent phenomenological description that connects nuclear interactions, elec-
tromagnetism, gravity, and certain quantum effects within a single hydrodynamic
framework. It also formulates new hypotheses and outlines experimental methods
for testing them.
1
Methodological Status and Evaluation Criteria
The proposed framework should be regarded as a phenomenological and heuristic
model rather than a fundamental theory. Its main value lies not in replacing existing
quantum field theories, but in providing a unified, visual picture and generating verifiable
predictions.
The evaluation criteria for this type of model are:
1. Internal consistency: Logical coherence between postulates and derived conclu-
sions.
2. External consistency: Ability to reproduce established experimental facts quan-
titatively or qualitatively (e.g., Coulomb’s law, nucleon magnetic moments).
3. Heuristic power: Capability to produce new, falsifiable hypotheses and predic-
tions.
4. Unifying capacity: Ability to integrate diverse phenomena into a coherent con-
ceptual system.

Nucleon Structure [2]
• Proton and neutron are modeled as two nested toroidal vortices with radii ∼0.25
fm and ∼1.3 fm.
• Magnetic moments arise naturally from rotational characteristics and agree quan-
titatively with experiment.
• The ratio of magnetic moments equals the ratio of toroidal radii, matching empirical
data (≈5.2).
• Suggested for educational use as a bridge between classical and quantum descrip-
tions.
• Experimental tests:
1. Measure the distribution of magnetization inside the nucleon, study excited
states.
2. Search for kinematic features in deep inelastic scattering processes.
2.3
Photon Model [3]
• The photon is treated as a vortex disturbance in the QV medium, with A as the
velocity field.
• The model reproduces the Jones formalism for polarization, maintaining internal
consistency.
• Predicts a small longitudinal component of the field, absent in standard QED.
• Experimental tests:
1. Direct detection of a longitudinal field along a collimated beam.
2. Observation of rotational recoil of single atoms during photon absorption.

2.4
Hydrodynamic Model of Gravitation [4]
• Gravitation arises from energy absorption of the QV by nucleons acting as sinks,
creating a pressure gradient.
• Qualitatively reproduces Newton’s law: F ∼m1m2/R2.
• Expresses G through vacuum parameters: speed of sound, density, and absorption
efficiency.
• Provides testable predictions: deviations from inverse-square law, Casimir-related
anomalies, and EM–gravity coupling.
• Experimental tests:
1. Search for anomalies in the behavior of particles near dense matter (direct
verification of the hypothesis of absorption of vacuum energy).
2. Search for deviations from the inverse square law at ultra-small distances.
3. Investigation of the influence of quantum fluctuations (for example, in Casimir’s
experiments) on gravitational interaction.
4. Search for short-term changes in the gravitational field during rapid changes
in the electromagnetic state of matter.
2.5
Hydrodynamic Representation of the Strong Force [5]
• Nucleon–nucleon interaction is modeled as vortex–vortex coupling in an incompress-
ible fluid.
• Exhibits both attraction and short-range repulsion (“hard core”) consistent with
nuclear forces.
• Includes spin dependence via vortex orientation, explaining saturation of nuclear
forces.
• Experimental tests:
1. The study of the nucleon-nucleon scattering spin dependence.
2. The search for anomalies in the stability of light nuclei.
3. The analysis of hydrodynamic correlations in relativistic collisions of heavy
ions.

Unified Picture of Interactions
The studies [1–5] together form a coherent hierarchy:
• Foundation: Hydrodynamic equivalents for electromagnetic quantities.
• Matter: Nucleons as stable vortex systems.
• Interactions:
3
1. Electromagnetic: direct interaction of velocity and pressure fields.
2. Strong: vortex coupling and geometric saturation.
3. Gravitational: pressure gradients from energy absorption.
• Quantum phenomena: Photons as vortex perturbations with quantized angular
momentum.
Thus, the model presents a unified hydrodynamic view of fundamental forces as
manifestations of a single underlying medium - the quantum vacuum.

Conclusion
The unified hydrodynamic model presented in [1–5] provides a coherent phenomenologi-
cal framework linking nuclear, electromagnetic, quantum, and gravitational phenomena
through classical fluid mechanics analogies.
The quantum vacuum is modeled as a weakly viscous fluid whose parameters con-
sistently reproduce known physical constants across multiple interaction regimes. The
model is falsifiable, suggests several experimental tests, and offers substantial heuristic
and educational value. While not a replacement for quantum field theory or general rel-
ativity, it proposes a unifying conceptual language that may guide future theoretical and
experimental work in the study of fundamental interactions.
