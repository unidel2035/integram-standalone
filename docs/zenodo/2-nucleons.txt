Model of the Internal Structure of Nucleons Based on
a Vortex Analogy:
A Self-Consistent Description of Magnetic Moments
Dmitry Losinets
August 2025
Abstract
This work develops a formal hydrodynamic analogy proposed in [5], offering its
self-consistent extension for describing the magnetic moments of elementary parti-
cles. Based on the analysis of experimental data on the electric charge distribution
and magnetic moments of nucleons, a model of their internal structure as a system of
coaxial toroidal vortices is constructed. The model demonstrates quantitative agree-
ment with key experimental parameters, offering an intuitive picture for understand-
ing microphysical processes. Concrete proposals for the experimental verification of
the model are developed. It is emphasized that the approach does not claim to be
fundamental but possesses significant potential for educational applications.
1
Introduction
Experimental studies of electron scattering on nucleons [1, 2], including the pioneering
work of Hofstadter and the review by Schopper [3], have revealed a complex internal
structure of the proton and neutron, indicating the presence of a compact core ("kernel")
and an extended envelope ("mantle") [4]. The standard interpretation of these data relies
on the quark model.
In this work, we propose an alternative, phenomenological approach, developing a for-
mal analogy between electrodynamics and the dynamics of vortices in an ideal fluid, as
presented in [5]. A key development is the self-consistent extension of this analogy from
the description of macroscopic fields to the modeling of intrinsic particle properties, in
particular, their magnetic moments. The aim of the work is to construct an intuitively un-
derstandable and mathematically self-consistent model that can serve as a bridge between
classical and quantum concepts in the educational process.
2
Theoretical Basis of the Model: Development of the
Formal Analogy
2.1
Mathematical Framework
The basis of the model is the development of a formal analogy in which the quantum
vacuum is considered as a medium with properties close to those of an ideal fluid, with
quantized vortex motion [5, 8]. Within this analogy:
1
• The electric charge of a particle, represented as a vortex formation, is modeled
by the expression:
q = 2ρV ω
(1)
where ρ is the medium density, V is the vortex volume, ω is the angular velocity of
rotation. This relation follows directly from the analogy with circulation quantiza-
tion in superfluid liquids [8, 13].
• The magnetic moment is the key extension of the model. We propose to describe
it by an expression analogous to the moment of force in rotational motion [6, 9]:
µ = ρV (ωr)2
(2)
This expression emphasizes a unified mechanism for generating charge and magnetic
moment through rotational motion, but through its different characteristics (linear
velocity at the vortex periphery ωr for the magnetic moment and angular velocity
for the charge).
2.2
Key Assumptions of the Model
1. Nucleons represent a system of two coaxial toroidal vortices, corresponding to the
experimentally observed regions: the core (radius ∼0.25 fm) and the mantle (outer
radius ∼1.3 fm). This two-scale structure is clearly visible in the charge distribution
data obtained from electron scattering experiments (see Fig. 1).
2. The total charge and magnetic properties of the nucleon are determined by the
superposition of contributions from both vortex components.
3. The geometric parameters of the vortices are fixed by experimental data on charge
distribution [4], which ensures the self-consistency of the model.

4
Discussion of Results and Educational Value
The presented model offers a unified and intuitively understandable description of three
key experimental facts: charge distribution, anomalous magnetic moments, and nucleon
sizes. The use of a classical hydrodynamic analogy allows for the visualization of complex
quantum objects, representing their internal structure in terms of vortex motion.
This provides a significant advantage for educational applications: the model
can be used to create visual aids and simulations, helping students form an initial intu-
itive understanding of hadron structure before moving on to the complex mathematical
apparatus of QCD. It is important to emphasize that the model is phenomenological
and does not claim to be fundamental, but serves as a convenient tool for describing
and systematizing experimental data.

5
Development Prospects and Experimental Verifica-
tion
To test the model and its further development, the following experiments are proposed:
1. Measurement of the magnetization distribution inside the nucleon. A key
prediction of the model is the different density of the magnetic moment in the core
and mantle. Modern experiments on polarized elastic electron scattering [10] or
with muons could provide data to test this assertion.
2. Study of excited states of nucleons (resonances). The model predicts that
such states may correspond to a change in the vortex configuration (e.g., a transition
between parameter sets A/B). Measurement of their electromagnetic transition form
factors [11] is a critically important test.
3. Search for kinematic features in deep-inelastic scattering. One might expect
that the transition between scattering on the compact core and the extended mantle
could manifest itself in sharper changes in cross-sections compared to predictions of
models based on parton distributions.
4. Observation of rotational effects in collisions. The vortex nature of nucleons
might manifest itself in specific particle correlations in the final state in high-energy
collisions [12].
5. Extension to other hadrons. Testing similar relations for hyperons and mesons
could serve as a test of the universality of the proposed approach.
6
Conclusion
The formal hydrodynamic analogy from [5] has been developed, and its self-consistent
extension for describing the magnetic moments of nucleons has been proposed.
The
constructed phenomenological model, based on the concept of a system of nested vortices,
demonstrates quantitative agreement with experimental data on charge distribution and
magnetic moments. The model offers an intuitive understanding of the microstructure
of nucleons, providing a significant advantage for educational applications and visual
modeling. Explicit proposals for the experimental verification of the model are formulated.
The approach is not a fundamental theory but serves as an effective tool for knowledge
systematization and education.
