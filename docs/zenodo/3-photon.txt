Vortex Photon Model: Program for Experimental
Verification
Dmitry Losinets
September 2025
Abstract
This work investigates a vortex-based model of the photon as an alternative ap-
proach to quantum electrodynamics (QED). While mathematically consistent with
the Jones formalism for polarization in describing transverse field components, the
model makes distinct, falsifiable predictions - primarily the existence of a small lon-
gitudinal component in electromagnetic waves in vacuum. The article focuses on
designing critical experiments to test these predictions, including direct detection
of longitudinal field components and analysis of angular momentum transfer during
photon absorption. Results from these experiments could either validate the model
or reinforce the standard QED framework.
1
Introduction
Standard quantum electrodynamics (QED) represents one of the most accurate and well-
verified physical theories, whose predictions have been brilliantly confirmed experimen-
tally [1, 2]. The mathematical apparatus of QED, based on the concept of gauge fields,
has no equal in its predictive power [3, 4]. However, its abstract nature and lack of in-
tuitive mechanical analogies for describing quantum objects such as photons continue to
stimulate the search for alternative interpretations and models that can bridge the gap
between mathematical formalism and intuitive understanding [5].
This work explores the possibility of mechanical description of photons within the
framework of a vortex model of the quantum vacuum, as systematically presented in
a series of articles [6, 7]. The fundamental hypothesis of this approach is that electro-
magnetic phenomena can be described as manifestations of the dynamics of a physical
medium [8, 9]. Specifically, the model postulates a correspondence between the vector
potential A and the velocity of motion of a small volume of this medium (V = A/REMHD,
where REMHD is a dimensional constant) [6, 10].
The aim of this article is not to refute QED, but to demonstrate that this model:
(1) is self-consistent within its framework - its mathematical description for transverse
field components reduces to the known Jones formalism for polarization [11]; (2) differs
from standard theory through fundamentally testable predictions, the most important
of which is the existence of a small longitudinal component in electromagnetic waves in
vacuum [12, 13]; and (3) is falsifiable - it offers specific experiments whose results can
unambiguously confirm or refute its key postulates [14].
Thus, the work focuses on developing and describing critical experiments designed to
test this hypothesis and quantitatively evaluate its predictions [15].

Justification of the Jones Formalism for the Pre-
sented Model
Consider a thin-walled vortex of radius R, whose center of mass moves with constant
velocity V . Each point of this vortex relative to the center of mass moves with constant
tangential velocity along a circle. The orientation of the vortex plane is arbitrary.
To describe the orientation of the vortex plane, we use two successive rotations:
1. Zero position (0,0): The vortex plane coincides with the XY plane. This means
that the normal to the vortex plane at the initial moment is directed along the
Z-axis, and the point with zero phase has coordinates (R, 0, 0).
2. First rotation (angle a): Rotation around the Z-axis by angle a. The new position
of the plane is described by the pair (a, 0).
3. Second rotation (angle b): Rotation of the already rotated plane around its new
X-axis (which coincides with the X-axis of the laboratory system after the first
rotation) by angle b. The final position is given by the pair (a, b).

Experimental Verification of the Model
4.1
Search for Longitudinal Component of Electromagnetic Field
Core contradiction:
• Standard model: Electromagnetic waves in vacuum are strictly transverse. The
vectors E and B oscillate perpendicular to the direction of wave propagation k.
There are no longitudinal components.
• Vortex model: The model explicitly predicts the presence of a longitudinal mo-
tion component (along the Z-axis), which implies the possibility of existence of a
longitudinal field component or oscillations.
Experiment description: Use a high-frequency antenna detector (e.g., for mi-
crowave or terahertz range) that is sensitive to electric field and place it directly on
the axis of a radiation beam propagating in a vacuum chamber.
Setup: Point radiation source (e.g., laser or maser) →Collimator (forms a narrow
beam) →Vacuum tube →Detector capable of moving along the beam axis with high
precision.
Procedure: The detector measures the field strength along the beam axis. According
to standard theory, when the detector is precisely aligned with the beam axis, it should not
5
detect any signal, since field oscillations occur perpendicular to this axis. Any detected
axial signal would indicate a longitudinal component.
Control: The experiment must be repeated for different polarization types (linear,
circular) from the source.
Expected result according to standard theory: The signal on the detector will
be absent or equal to the noise level (caused by, for example, scattering on residual gases
or non-ideal collimation). This would refute the vortex model.
Expected result according to vortex model: The detector will register an oscil-
latory signal at the radiation frequency, indicating the presence of a longitudinal electric
field component.
4.2
Violation of Conservation Laws at photon creation/annihilation
Core contradiction:
• Standard model: A photon is a massless particle with spin 1, obeying conservation
laws of energy, momentum, and angular momentum. Its creation and absorption
are point-like interaction acts in quantum field theory.
• Vortex model: If a photon is an extended rotating object with internal structure,
then during its absorption by a single atom (e.g., in the photoelectric effect), not
only should a quantum of energy be transferred but also the angular momentum
of this vortex. This could violate symmetry and conservation laws in elementary
processes.
Experiment description: Investigate recoil during absorption of a single photon.
Setup: An ion or atom cooled to ultralow temperatures and trapped in a trap.
A source of single photons (e.g., quantum dot) emitting photons with known angular
momentum (circularly polarized).
Procedure: Observe the state of the ion after photon absorption.
According to
standard theory, the ion should only receive recoil along the direction of the photon’s
momentum (p = ℏk). If the photon possesses additional "vortex" angular momentum,
this should cause rotational recoil - the ion should start rotating around its axis after
absorption.
Control: Carefully measure changes in both linear and angular momentum of the
ion with utmost precision.
Expected result according to standard theory: The change in the ion’s angular
momentum will be equal to the photon’s spin momentum (±ℏ) but will not be caused by
mechanical transfer of momentum from an extended vortex. No anomalous rotation will
be observed. This would refute the vortex model.
Expected result according to vortex model: Photon absorption will cause mea-
surable rotation of the ion, whose magnitude may depend on the "size" or "shape" of the
vortex predicted by the model.

Conclusion
In this work, we have analyzed the vortex photon model based on the idea of corre-
spondence between the vector potential and the velocity of elements of a hypothetical
medium. We have shown that for transverse field components, the model is mathemati-
cally equivalent to the standard Jones formalism, ensuring its internal consistency in this
regard.
The main result of this article is not the model itself, but the development of a
methodology for its experimental verification. We have formulated critical differences
between the model and standard QED and proposed two specific experimental schemes
aimed at their detection: (1) direct search for longitudinal components of the electromag-
netic field, and (2) identification of specific effects of angular momentum transfer during
photon absorption.
It is important to emphasize that standard QED unambiguously predicts negative re-
sults in these experiments. Therefore, any registered positive effect corresponding to the
model’s quantitative predictions would have revolutionary significance for physics. Con-
versely, the absence of expected effects within achievable measurement accuracy would
mean falsification of this particular vortex interpretation.
Even in the latter case, the proposed experiments retain their scientific value, as they
are aimed at testing fundamental principles (transverse nature of waves, photon angular
momentum) at new levels of precision and under new conditions. The presented model
thus serves not so much as an alternative theory but as a heuristic tool generating new,
potentially interesting experimental tasks.
Future work should involve conducting quantitative calculations of expected effect
magnitudes (e.g., amplitude of longitudinal components for photons of different energies)
and directly organizing experimental studies that will serve as the ultimate arbiter in
assessing the validity of the proposed approach.
